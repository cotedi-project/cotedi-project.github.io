## ABC Thinking

**Associação TreeTree2, with Universidad Rey Juan Carlos**

*ABC Thinking* (A Bebras Computational Thinking programme) is a programme of 12 weekly one-hour lessons for 3rd and 4th grade. In each lesson, students solve Bebras-style tasks on paper about a computational thinking topic, such as stacks and queues, binary numbers, graphs or finite state machines. No computer is needed: only the printed tasks and a pencil. The materials are in Portuguese.

### Introduction

Bebras tasks are short problems, set in everyday situations, that can be solved without any prior knowledge of computing but that require the kind of reasoning used in computer science. *ABC Thinking* organises these tasks into a weekly programme, so that teachers can work on computational thinking regularly with the whole class, using only paper and pencil.

### Activity design

Every lesson has three to eight multiple-choice tasks and includes:

* **Preparation for the teacher**: the goal of the session and how to run its tasks.
* **Fully explained solutions**, so the teacher can discuss each task with the class.
* **A student edition** to print, without the teacher-only parts.

The 12 lessons are:

1. *First tasks*: a gentle introduction with three typical Bebras tasks of increasing difficulty.
2. *Stacks and queues*: four tasks to build a practical intuition about these data structures.
3. *Abstraction and patterns*: four tasks on abstraction and pattern recognition.
4. *Logic puzzles*: four puzzles in which students check conditions and constraints, keeping track of the information on paper.
5. *Mini-test*: six tasks of different types, solved against the clock.
6. *Instructions*: three tasks on sequences of commands that must say exactly what to do.
7. *Binary numbers*: four tasks on representing information with only two symbols, 0 and 1.
8. *Second mini-test*: eight tasks from earlier lessons, now against the clock.
9. *Graphs*: three harder tasks on a way of representing relationships between objects.
10. *Conditions and logical expressions*: four tasks on the basis of any program.
11. *Finite state machines*: four tasks on models that represent programs and circuits.
12. *Final mini-competition*: eight tasks from earlier lessons, in a competition to close the programme.

The three mini-tests (lessons 5, 8 and 12) let the teacher follow the students' progress.

### Results

*ABC Thinking* was evaluated in a study published in 2024 in *Education and Information Technologies*. The study applied a computational thinking test, the cCTt (*competent Computational Thinking test*), before and after the programme, and the results showed a significant development of specific computational thinking skills. The qualitative data from the study suggest that the programme fits teachers' practice.

## Conclusion

*ABC Thinking* offers primary school teachers a ready-to-use, low-cost way of working on computational thinking every week, with tasks that need no computer and full support for the teacher in every lesson.
